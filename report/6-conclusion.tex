We asked whether fresh, firm-level earnings-call sentiment improves cross-sectional prediction of monthly excess stock returns. Within our framework, the answer is a qualified yes: after freshness filtering ($\leq 3$ months), delta feature construction, and cross-sectional standardisation, a FinBERT-only XGBoost model delivers Rank IC $t$-statistic 2.67 over 108 out-of-sample months and $+11.4\%$ net annualised return (Sharpe 0.79) over the 2019--2023 test period. A cross-sectional MLP on the same features confirms the signal with $+7.87\%$ net (Sharpe 0.58), while a Ridge linear benchmark fails (Sharpe $-0.10$), indicating the signal is mildly non-linear.

The preprocessing ablation shows that delta features (quarter-over-quarter sentiment changes) are the single most impactful engineering step --- consistent with \cite{huang2023} and with the interpretation that the market prices the sentiment level quickly but adjusts more slowly to tone changes. JKP factor returns are not well aligned with this cross-sectional strategy design: being constant across stocks within a month, they serve time-series rather than cross-sectional purposes and degrade portfolio performance when combined with FinBERT. Sequence models (LSTM, Transformer) generalise well pre-COVID but fail in the post-COVID regime, suggesting that quarterly earnings-call trajectories are too sparse and regime-dependent for robust out-of-sample prediction.

The results are consistent with the hypothesis that fresh changes in earnings-call tone contain incremental information not fully captured by our benchmarks. We caution that the signal is modest (monthly IC $\approx 0.02$), the test period is short (60 months), and the covered universe is biased toward mid-and large-cap firms with regular earnings calls. Future work could explore higher-frequency signal updates, alternative NLP models, or firm-level fundamental conditioning to improve both coverage and signal stability.
