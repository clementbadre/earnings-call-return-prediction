This paper demonstrates that fresh earnings-call sentiment, engineered with quarter-over-quarter delta features and cross-sectional standardisation, provides a statistically significant and economically meaningful cross-sectional signal for excess stock returns. Our four key contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item A \textbf{freshness filter} ($\leq 3$ months) that eliminates stale signals and recovers predictive power that is entirely absent in the raw, unfiltered sentiment data. Without this filter, median signal staleness of 7 months renders FinBERT scores essentially uninformative.
    \item A \textbf{structural diagnosis} of JKP quantitative factors: their within-month constancy makes them time-series rather than cross-sectional predictors, and combining them with FinBERT degrades rather than improves portfolio performance due to signal crowding and higher transaction costs. This finding is model-agnostic: it holds equally for XGBoost and deep learning models.
    \item A \textbf{unified XGBoost vs.\ deep learning ablation} across eight models. A cross-sectional MLP on FinBERT features (IC 0.013, Sharpe 0.58) confirms mild non-linearities and validates the signal across model classes. Temporal sequence models (LSTM, Attention) fail out-of-sample due to regime non-stationarity --- the cross-sectional rank of current sentiment is more robust than its historical trajectory.
    \item A \textbf{robust out-of-sample evaluation} spanning 108 months (2015--2023) with explicit transaction costs, multi-period sub-period analysis, and a formal IC $t$-test ($t = 2.67$, $p < 0.01$).
\end{itemize}

The FinBERT-only XGBoost long-short decile strategy delivers $+11.4\%$ net annualised return, Sharpe ratio 0.79, and alpha $+10.6\%$ over the 2019--2023 out-of-sample period. The PyTorch MLP on the same features delivers $+7.87\%$ net, Sharpe 0.58 --- confirming the signal's robustness to model architecture. Performance is positive in all three sub-periods (pre-COVID: $+3.9\%$; COVID: $+15.0\%$; post-COVID: $+12.7\%$), with the strongest risk-adjusted returns observed after the COVID-19 shock.

These results suggest that the informational content of earnings-call communication --- particularly its \textit{change} relative to prior quarters --- is not yet fully priced by the market, even in the post-2019 period. The cross-sectional nature of this signal, combined with the relatively low turnover of sentiment-based portfolios, makes it a promising complement to quantitative factor strategies in practice. Future work could investigate whether combining the FinBERT cross-sectional signal with a high-frequency rebalancing mechanism or with company-specific fundamental data further improves risk-adjusted performance.
