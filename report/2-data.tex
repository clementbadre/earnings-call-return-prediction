\subsection{CRSP Monthly Returns}

We use the CRSP monthly stock file for January 2008 to December 2023 (1,450,439 raw observations). The target variable is the monthly excess return:
\[
    r^e_{i,t} = \text{MthRet}_{i,t} - \text{sprtrn}_t
\]
where $\text{sprtrn}_t$ is the S\&P500 return. Raw returns are winsorised at the 1st/99th percentiles. The excess return has mean $-0.019\%$ per month and 48.2\% positive observations, confirming it is centred at zero.

\subsection{JKP Quantitative Factor Returns}

We use 153 long-short factor portfolio returns from \cite{jkp2023} (momentum, value, quality, profitability, investment, low-risk). These are monthly time-series signals: each factor takes the same value for all stocks in a given month. \textbf{Design choice:} JKP factor returns for period $t$ are realised over the same interval as CRSP returns; using them contemporaneously would be look-ahead. We lag them by one month before merging, so the vector at $t-1$ is used to predict CRSP return at $t$.

This lagging implies that JKP features encode \textit{market-level} information about recent factor performance. They may help with time-series allocation or regime identification, but they do not vary \textit{across firms} within a given month and are therefore not naturally suited to cross-sectional stock selection.

\subsection{Earnings Calls and FinBERT Inference}

We obtained 34,643 earnings call transcripts matched to CRSP firms by PERMNO. We extract the Q\&A section (most forward-looking), chunk into 512-token segments, and score each chunk with \texttt{ProsusAI/finbert} \cite{araci2019}, a RoBERTa model fine-tuned on financial text. Each call yields three probabilities averaged over chunks: $p_\text{pos}$, $p_\text{neg}$, $p_\text{neu}$ (summing to unity). Calls with fewer than 100 words are excluded. Inference details and score distributions are in Appendix~\ref{app:finbert}.

\subsection{Feature Engineering}

From the three raw FinBERT scores we construct the following features:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Engineered FinBERT features. Delta features are NaN for a firm's first earnings call.}
\label{tab:features}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
\begin{tabular}{>{\ttfamily}l p{9cm}}
\toprule
\normalfont\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{Definition} \\
\midrule
net\_sentiment      & $p_\text{pos} - p_\text{neg}$: signed directional signal \\
sentiment\_strength & $1 - p_\text{neu}$: non-neutrality of the call \\
delta\_net          & $\Delta\,\texttt{net\_sentiment}$ quarter-over-quarter (\textit{surprise signal}) \\
delta\_pos, delta\_neg & Quarter-over-quarter changes in individual components \\
months\_since\_call & Signal age (1, 2 or 3 by construction after freshness filter) \\
*\_zscore           & Cross-sectional z-score within each prediction month \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Freshness filter.} The raw merged dataset reveals a severe staleness problem: the median lag between the most recent earnings call and the prediction date is \textbf{7 months}, at which point the sentiment signal has largely decayed. We retain only stock-month pairs where the most recent call occurred within \textbf{3 months} of the prediction date, reducing the dataset from 517,776 to \textbf{122,847 observations}. Section~\ref{sec:results} shows this filter is the most critical preprocessing step.

\textbf{Universe note.} The JKP-only model is evaluated on a broader universe (40,872 test observations) since it does not require earnings-call data. FinBERT-containing models are restricted to firms with a recent earnings call ($\leq$3 months) and, for delta features, at least two consecutive calls (36,901 test observations). Results for FinBERT models therefore apply to an earnings-call-covered universe (predominantly mid- and large-cap firms). Cross-model comparisons within Table~\ref{tab:ablation} are partially confounded by this universe difference.

\textbf{Cross-sectional z-scoring} removes time-varying means and scales, preventing the model from learning calendar effects that cannot be reproduced out-of-sample. \textbf{Delta features} are computed strictly using prior-quarter call data, with no look-ahead. The scaler is fitted on the training set only and applied to validation and test sets.

\subsection{Temporal Split}

We apply a strict temporal split with no random shuffling:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Train}: January 2008 -- December 2014 (52,931 obs)
    \item \textbf{Validation} (early stopping only): January 2015 -- December 2018 (29,044 obs)
    \item \textbf{Test} (all reported metrics): January 2019 -- December 2023 (40,872 obs)
\end{itemize}
