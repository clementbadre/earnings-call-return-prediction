Earnings conference calls are public information events in which managers discuss results, strategy, and outlook. Related financial-text evidence suggests that recent tone changes can carry more predictive content than sentiment levels alone, because levels are partly anticipated by the market \cite{huang2023}. We ask a precise question: \textbf{does fresh, firm-level earnings-call sentiment improve cross-sectional prediction of monthly excess stock returns beyond simple quantitative benchmarks, and if so, through which feature-engineering choices?}

We address this question using FinBERT \cite{araci2019} sentiment scores from 34,643 earnings calls matched to CRSP monthly returns over 2008--2023. Our contribution is primarily empirical and methodological: we document that signal freshness, quarter-over-quarter delta features, and cross-sectional standardisation together convert a weak raw sentiment signal into a modest, tradable cross-sectional predictor. We benchmark against 153 JKP factor-return signals \cite{jkp2023} and compare XGBoost with a deep learning MLP across the same features.

\subsection*{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{A fresh FinBERT signal:} freshness filter ($\leq 3$ months), quarter-over-quarter delta features, and cross-sectional z-scoring. A preprocessing ablation shows each step is necessary; cross-sectional z-scoring is the most impactful for portfolio performance.
    \item \textbf{Model comparison:} XGBoost and four PyTorch deep learning models --- two MLPs on the 12 tabular FinBERT features, one LSTM and one Transformer on 8-quarter FinBERT score sequences. MLP\textsubscript{FinBERT} achieves positive net returns and Sharpe, confirming the DL component is empirically productive rather than decorative; sequence models do not generalise across regimes.
    \item \textbf{Economic evaluation:} long-short decile backtests with 0.2\% one-way transaction costs, multi-period robustness, and a 108-month post-training IC analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Main Findings}

In our tests, the only signal that survives both the IC and backtest checks is fresh FinBERT sentiment. The FinBERT-only XGBoost long-short decile yields $+11.4\%$ net annualised return (Sharpe 0.79) over 2019--2023, with a Rank IC $t$-statistic of 3.79 over the 108-month post-training window. A cross-sectional MLP on the same features produces $+7.87\%$ net (Sharpe 0.58), so the signal is not tied to XGBoost alone. This is a modest signal, not a large forecasting edge.

JKP factor returns, being identical across all stocks within a given month, are structurally suited to time-series or market-timing applications rather than firm-level cross-sectional stock selection. Their poor long-short performance in our setup reflects a design mismatch, not a shortcoming of the JKP literature. The results are consistent with the hypothesis that fresh changes in earnings-call tone contain incremental information not fully captured by our benchmarks, though we caution that the test period (60 months) leaves meaningful statistical uncertainty.
