\subsection{Ablation Study}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} present out-of-sample metrics on the 2019--2023 test set.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Ablation study --- out-of-sample test period (January 2019 -- December 2023).}
\label{tab:ablation}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{$R^2$} & \textbf{Rank IC} \\
\midrule
JKP only                  & $-0.016$ & $0.034$ \\
FinBERT only               & $-0.013$ & $0.009$ \\
JKP + FinBERT              & $-0.013$ & $0.051$ \\
JKP + FinBERT $\times$10   & $-0.012$ & $\mathbf{0.072}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Adding FinBERT to JKP improves global Rank IC by 50\% (0.051 vs.\ 0.034). The feature-weighted variant raises IC further to 0.072. The negative $R^2$ values are expected and reflect the near-unpredictability of individual stock excess returns in absolute terms --- this is a property of financial markets, not a modelling failure.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig7_ablation.pdf}
    \caption{Ablation study: Rank IC (left), net annualised return of LS Decile (centre), and net Sharpe ratio (right). High global Rank IC does not necessarily translate into strong backtest performance --- see Section~5.}
    \label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Structural finding on JKP.} When evaluated using \textit{monthly} Rank IC (one Spearman correlation per month, cross-sectional), JKP-only yields NaN. The 153 JKP factors are identical for all stocks in a given month; the model therefore produces a constant prediction within each month, making cross-sectional ranking undefined. \textbf{JKP factors are time-series signals (market timing) and cannot perform cross-sectional stock selection by design.}
