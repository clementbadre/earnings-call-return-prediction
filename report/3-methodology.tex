\subsection{Feature Engineering}

Beyond raw FinBERT scores, we construct the following features motivated by the finance NLP literature:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Engineered FinBERT features. Delta features are NaN for a firm's first earnings call.}
\label{tab:features}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
\begin{tabular}{>{\ttfamily}l p{9.5cm}}
\toprule
\normalfont\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{Definition and motivation} \\
\midrule
net\_sentiment      & $p_\text{pos} - p_\text{neg}$: signed directional signal \\
sentiment\_strength & $1 - p_\text{neu}$: non-neutrality of the call \\
delta\_net          & $\Delta\,\texttt{net\_sentiment}$ quarter-over-quarter --- captures earnings surprises \cite{huang2023} \\
delta\_pos, delta\_neg & Quarter-over-quarter changes in individual sentiment components \\
months\_since\_call & Signal freshness (1, 2 or 3 by construction after filter) \\
*\_zscore           & Cross-sectional z-score within each prediction month for \texttt{net\_sentiment}, \texttt{delta\_net}, \texttt{delta\_pos} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Cross-sectional z-scoring removes time-varying means and scales, preventing the model from learning calendar effects that cannot be reproduced out-of-sample.

\subsection{Temporal Train / Validation / Test Split}

We apply a strict temporal split with no random shuffling:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Train}: January 2008 -- December 2014
    \item \textbf{Validation} (early stopping only): January 2015 -- December 2018
    \item \textbf{Test} (all reported metrics): January 2019 -- December 2023
\end{itemize}
A \texttt{StandardScaler} is fitted on the training set and applied to validation and test sets.

\subsection{Models: XGBoost Ablation Study}

We train four XGBoost regressors targeting $r^e_{i,t}$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{JKP only}: 153 quantitative factor features.
    \item \textbf{FinBERT only}: 12 sentiment features (Table~\ref{tab:features}).
    \item \textbf{JKP + FinBERT}: all 165 features.
    \item \textbf{JKP + FinBERT $\times$10}: same 165 features but FinBERT columns receive $10\times$ higher sampling probability via XGBoost's \texttt{feature\_weights} parameter in \texttt{DMatrix}, biasing each split toward sentiment features.
\end{enumerate}

All models share the same hyperparameters: 500 estimators, early stopping at 30 rounds (validation RMSE), learning rate 0.05, max depth 4, subsampling 0.8, $L_2$ regularisation 1.0.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{$R^2$}: fraction of variance explained. Expected near zero for individual excess returns.
    \item \textbf{Rank IC}: Spearman rank correlation between predicted and realised excess returns. The standard performance metric in cross-sectional equity research \cite{grinold1999}.
    \item \textbf{IC $t$-statistic}: $\bar{\text{IC}} / (\sigma_\text{IC} / \sqrt{T})$ over monthly ICs --- tests whether the signal is statistically distinguishable from noise.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Investment Strategies and Transaction Costs}

Three equal-weighted strategies are implemented monthly:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{LS Decile}: long top 10\%, short bottom 10\%.
    \item \textbf{LS Quintile}: long top 20\%, short bottom 20\%.
    \item \textbf{LO Decile}: long top 10\% only.
\end{itemize}

Transaction costs of \textbf{0.2\% one-way} (0.4\% round-trip) are applied on monthly turnover. We report annualised gross and net returns, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, OLS alpha, and beta versus the S\&P500.
